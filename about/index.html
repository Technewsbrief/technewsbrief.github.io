About Tech News Brief

Tech News Brief is a short, sourced digest of the day's most important technology news. It's for curious non-specialists who want the big changes and why they matter, without the noise. Our aim is to be accurate, not first. It is run independently by Damien Conway in Ireland.

How stories are chosen

Several times a day we check 14 outlets: Ars Technica, BBC Technology, Engadget, Hacker News (credited to the site each item links to), IEEE Spectrum, KrebsOnSecurity, MIT Technology Review, Quanta Magazine, Rest of World, TechCrunch, The Register, The Verge, Tom's Hardware and Wired. Articles about the same event are grouped, and each event is scored for how much it matters to a general audience.

We favour AI and its effect on society and work; policy, law, privacy and security; science and big engineering; and consumer tech that changes how people live or work. Minor product updates, rumours, deals, reviews and opinion pieces rank low or are left out. If nothing is important enough, we post nothing.

How summaries are written

Summaries are written by AI (Anthropic's Claude) from the full text of up to three articles from different outlets. The rules:

  • Only facts stated in those articles are used, never the AI's own background knowledge.
  • Facts that several outlets agree on come first. A fact from a single outlet is credited to it ("according to…").
  • Details the outlets disagree on are left out.
  • If there isn't enough readable text, for example because the article is behind a paywall, the story is skipped rather than guessed at.

Posts are published automatically, without a person checking each one first. That's why every post links to its sources: the original reporting is the authority, and we recommend reading it.

Images and video

Post images are text cards showing the headline and key points, not AI-generated pictures. Reels are narrated by an AI voice and may use stock footage from Pixabay, which is labelled in the caption.

What we don't do

We don't get around paywalls, we don't copy articles, and we don't write clickbait or ask for likes.

Corrections and contact

If we get something wrong, email hello@technewsbrief.net or send a message to @technews.brief on Threads or Instagram, and we'll correct or remove the post.
